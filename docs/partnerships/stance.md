# Clawrari collaborates, not competes.

Clawrari is a small project in a crowded neighborhood. Claude Skills is a platform with thousands of authors. obra/superpowers, basic-memory, Letta, and a half-dozen "agent OS" projects all sit within a few clicks of us. We will not win by pretending they don't exist or by positioning ourselves as their replacement.

We win by being a good neighbor.

## Why this is the right stance, now

Clawrari is at the stage where attention is the scarce resource and credibility is the currency. At this stage:

- Picking fights with adjacent projects is the cheapest way to get noticed and the most expensive way to lose long-term trust.
- The people we want as users — operators who care about durable systems — are the same people who notice when a project punches sideways at its peers.
- The maintainers of those adjacent projects are a small, well-connected group. They talk. A reputation as collegial or as petty both compound.
- We are not yet good enough to compete on prestige. We **are** good enough to ship something useful and credit the people we learned from.

## What collaboration looks like in practice

1. **Contribute upstream when we can.** If we write a skill that fits cleanly into obra/superpowers, we PR it there too. If we find a bug in basic-memory while integrating, we file an issue with a repro. Our public diffs to other projects are themselves a credibility signal.

2. **Credit by default.** When we adopt a pattern from another project, the docs say so by name with a link. "Inspired by X" is cheap and earns goodwill. Pretending we invented it earns the opposite.

3. **Cross-promotion is mutual, never extractive.** We will not ask another maintainer for a shoutout unless we have already given them one. We will not write "alternatives" posts that mention competitors only to dunk on them.

4. **Contribute before we ask.** A first PR or a first piece of public praise should precede any "want to collaborate?" message by at least a week. Otherwise the ask is the relationship.

5. **Fork only when contribution fails.** If we need behavior an upstream project won't accept, we fork narrowly, document why, and keep the door open to merge back. We do not fork-and-rebrand to avoid attribution.

## The hard line — when collaboration becomes assimilation

Collaboration has a failure mode: we slowly shape Clawrari to please an adjacent project's maintainers until our own point of view goes dull. That is worse than competing.

We say no — politely, in the open — when:

- A peer asks us to remove or soften a core principle (files-first, no hidden memory, internal boldness + external restraint) to fit their model.
- An integration request would couple Clawrari to infrastructure that violates our license or operating principles (hosted-as-source-of-truth, paid-only dependencies).
- A merge or rename proposal would dissolve Clawrari into another project's brand. We can be a recommended setup; we are not a feature of someone else's repo.
- A cross-promo arrangement requires us to misrepresent what Clawrari is or to mute critique of approaches we disagree with.

The shape of the no: name the constraint, thank the person, propose the next-best thing we *can* do. No ghosting, no public sniping, no quiet drift. If we have to walk away from a partnership, we do it once, clearly, and then continue to credit them in our docs when their work earns it.

Clawrari is a small project today. It can grow without elbows out.
